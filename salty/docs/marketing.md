# Salty marketing playbook

How to get Salty in front of 18 to 30 year olds without spending money, and how to tell within two weeks whether the idea has legs. Read `brand.md` first for voice and look.

All figures quoted below are real: UK full-time yearly pay before tax, from the Office for National Statistics 2026 provisional release, as loaded into the site. Re-check them against the site before posting, because the data is refreshed each year.

## The plan in one paragraph

Two accounts post short videos every day for two weeks. A **Salty account** posts pay reveals built straight from the data. A **founder account** in your own name tells the story of building it. Every video sends people to the free pay check, the check produces a share image that sends more people, and the waitlist counts how many of them want the paid app. If the numbers at the end clear the bar below, build the app. If not, we have lost two weeks and no money.

## Why this can work for free

- **The data is the content.** There are about 360 UK job groups on the site. Each one is a video and a web page. You never run out.
- **The demo is the ad.** Typing a job and a salary and getting a giant number back works as a silent five-second clip.
- **Everyone has a job to check.** The audience is not a niche; the hook is their own job title.
- **The share image carries the address.** People post their rank, and the image tells their friends where to check theirs.

## Accounts to set up

| Account | Posts | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Salty (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts) | Pay reveals, demos, comment replies | Reach, and trust in the number |
| Founder, in your name (same three, plus X or LinkedIn if you like) | Build-in-public | People follow people; this is the personal brand everything funnels to |

Use the same handle everywhere if you can get it. Put the site address in each bio. Link the founder account from the Salty bio and the other way round.

## Six video formats

**1. Which pays more?** Two jobs on screen, three seconds to guess, then the reveal. Built from data, no face needed, and people argue in the comments.

> "Which pays more in the UK: train driver or solicitor?" … "Train driver. £74,376 against £57,198."
>
> "Scaffolder or architect?" … "Scaffolder, just. £48,764 against £47,665."

**2. The gap inside one job.** Same job title, wildly different pay.

> "Two software developers, same job title. One is on £33,000. The other is on £114,000. Both are normal. Which one are you?"

**3. The five-second demo.** Screen recording: type a job, type a salary, the number lands. Caption only, trending audio.

> "I checked my pay against every nurse in the country." (Result: "66% are paid more than you.")

**4. Comment your job.** "Comment your job and I'll tell you what it really pays." Then reply to comments with a video each. This is the engine: every reply is a new video aimed at someone who asked for it, and their whole trade sees it.

**5. Reply to the street interview.** Stitch or react to a "what do you earn?" clip with the official figure for that job. Credit the original creator.

**6. Building it (founder account only).** Short, honest updates: what was built today, the sign-up count, what went wrong. Day-numbered so people follow the series.

> "Day 4 of building an app that tells you if you're underpaid. [Your real number] people checked their pay yesterday. Here's the job that surprised me most."

## Hooks that fit the data

Use these as the first line on screen. Each is true for the UK figures today.

1. "Train drivers earn more than lawyers. Here's the number." (£74,376 against £57,198 for solicitors)
2. "The typical care worker earns £28,738. The typical warehouse worker earns £29,379."
3. "A quarter of chefs earn under £27,433."
4. "Teaching assistants are among the lowest paid full-time jobs in Britain: £22,018."
5. "One in ten software developers earns under £33,352. One in ten earns over £114,051."
6. "Police officers and paramedics earn almost exactly the same. Guess the number." (£52,569 and £52,875)
7. "If you're a nurse on less than £37,956, three quarters of the main nursing group out-earn you."
8. "The jobs where nobody tells you the pay, ranked."
9. "I typed in my salary and I wish I hadn't."
10. "You're probably underpaid. It takes ten seconds to find out."

## The loops that make it grow

- **Share image.** Shown after every check. It displays the rank and never the pay, so people post it.
- **"Copy link for a friend."** Sends a colleague straight to the page for the same job.
- **Job pages.** Every job has its own page ("Chefs: what the job pays") that search engines can find. This is slow but free and it compounds.
- **Comment-your-job replies.** Each one reaches a new trade.

## Places beyond short video

- **Trade communities.** Nurses, teachers, tradespeople and hospitality workers all have big groups and subreddits. Read each group's rules first and answer real pay questions with the figure and the source. Do not drop links where links are banned.
- **Data stories for the press.** "The ten jobs where the gap between the lowest and highest paid is widest" is a story a local paper or a newsletter will run with a link. One good story a month is enough.
- **Colleges and sixth forms.** Careers advisers need "what does this job pay" and the site answers it for free.

## The two-week test

**What to do:** two Salty videos a day and one founder video a day for 14 days. That is about 40 videos. Use formats 1 to 3 in week one, and move to comment replies as soon as comments arrive.

**What to measure** (the site is set up to count each of these once analytics is switched on):

| Step | What it tells you | My starting target |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors who complete a pay check | Does the page deliver on the video's promise? | 40% or more |
| Checks that end in a waitlist sign-up | Do people want the paid app? | 8% or more |
| Checks that end in a share | Will it spread on its own? | 5% or more |
| Waitlist sign-ups in 14 days | Is there enough demand to build? | 300 or more |
| Which waitlist feature gets picked most | What to build first | Whichever leads clearly |

These targets are my own starting points for a go or no-go decision, not industry benchmarks. Adjust them after the first week of real numbers. The clearest single signal is the feature people pick on the waitlist form: if most choose "Pay by company", build that; if most choose the script, build that.

**After two weeks:**

- Targets met: build the paid app, starting with the most-picked feature.
- Views but no checks: the videos work and the landing page does not. Fix the page.
- Checks but no sign-ups: people like the free tool and do not want more. Rethink what is paid.
- No views: change the formats before changing the product. Try five new hooks.

## What it costs

Nothing for the test, as built. The site is plain files that free hosting can serve, the data is free, and the videos are made from the data. The first real costs are a domain name (around the price of a few coffees a year) and, later, Apple's developer fee if there is an iPhone app.
