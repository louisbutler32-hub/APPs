# How Salty makes money

Six ways Salty can earn, in the order they become possible, and how each one feeds your personal brand. Prices here are starting guesses to test, not researched price points.

## The shape of it

```
Short videos (Salty account + your founder account)
        │
        ▼
Free pay check  ──►  share image  ──►  more people
        │
        ▼
Waitlist / email list  ◄── you own this; it is the asset
        │
        ├─► 1. The paid app
        ├─► 2. A script pack you can sell before the app exists
        ├─► 3. A weekly newsletter with sponsors
        ├─► 4. Recommended tools (affiliate)
        ├─► 5. Pay reports for small employers
        └─► 6. You: the founder who built it in public
```

The free check is never paid. It is the advert, and charging for it would kill the sharing.

## 1. The paid app

The free check tells people where they stand. The paid app tells them what to do about it. The four candidate features are the ones on the waitlist form, and the form records which one each person wants, so demand decides what gets built first.

| Feature | Why someone pays |
|---|---|
| Pay by company | The real question is "what does *my* employer pay?" |
| The ask-for-more script | A word-for-word script built from their own numbers |
| Offer check | High-stakes moment: a job offer is on the table today |
| Pay alerts | A reason to stay subscribed after the first check |

Pricing to test: a small monthly subscription, a cheaper yearly one, or a one-off "raise pack" for people who only need it once. A pay check is occasional by nature, so a one-off price may beat a subscription. Test it with a price page before building billing.

## 2. A script pack, sold now

A short guide: how to ask for a raise, with fill-in-the-blank scripts for the three common situations (pay review, new offer, found out a colleague earns more). It can be sold under your own name through a free creator-store page before the app exists. It earns a little, proves people will pay for the outcome, and later becomes the app's script feature.

## 3. A weekly newsletter

One email a week: three surprising pay facts from the data and one practical tip. It keeps the waitlist warm so they still remember Salty at launch, it is yours whatever happens to any social platform, and once it has a few thousand readers it can carry a sponsor. Send it from you, by name, with Salty as the thing you make.

## 4. Recommended tools

People who discover they are underpaid often want a better CV, a course or a new job. Recommending good ones, with a clear "we may earn a commission" label, earns per sign-up. Only recommend things you would use; one bad recommendation costs more trust than it earns.

## 5. Pay reports for small employers

The same data, pointed the other way: a small business wants to know what to offer for a role so it does not lose staff. A one-page report per role is something a business will pay more for than an individual will. This comes later, once the consumer side has an audience, and it needs no new data.

## 6. You

Building in public turns the project into your reputation. The founder account's audience follows you to the next thing, whether Salty becomes a business or not. Practical rules so the value stays with you:

- The email list and the accounts are registered to you, not to a tool or an agency.
- The founder account links to Salty and to your own page, and Salty's site has a "built in public" block that links back (it appears once your handles are set).
- Post the real numbers, including the bad days. That is what people follow.

## What is built into the site today

| Path | State in the product |
|---|---|
| Email list | Asked for once when the site opens (easy to close, stays away for a month), and offered again at each step |
| Raise plan | Built. Six questions, then a free plan: the figure to ask for, how strong the case is, when to ask, the opening line. The full word-for-word plan is locked behind payment, or collects emails until a payment link is set |
| Offer check | Built and free. Ranks a job offer and gives the counter figure and line |
| Employers | Built and free. Shows the low, fair and strong figure for a role and collects work emails for the full report |
| Recommended tools | The slot is built and hidden until real partners are added |
| Pay by company | Not built. No free source of company-level pay has been loaded |
| Countries not yet live | Shown as "soon" with the honest reason and an email sign-up, so demand is counted by country |

The order on screen follows one rule: give something useful first, then ask. The pay check is free, the raise plan summary is free, and only the full plan asks for money.

## What to do first

1. Run the two-week test in `marketing.md` and watch which waitlist feature wins.
2. Write the script pack while the test runs; sell it from your founder page.
3. Start the weekly email as soon as the list passes a hundred people.
4. Build the paid feature the waitlist asked for.
